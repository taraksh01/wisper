# how it works

From voice to text in one breath

A short pipeline with no round trip to a server. You speak, a local model listens, your words land.

  1. 01

    Speak

    Hold the global hotkey for push-to-talk, or tap it to toggle. Your mic records locally, and the overlay pill shows a live waveform.

  2. 02

    Transcribe

    Local ONNX engines: Parakeet, Moonshine, or IndicConformer. They turn speech into text on your machine, silence is trimmed, and models stay loaded so your next dictation starts instantly.

  3. 03

    Insert

    The text is typed at your cursor or copied to the clipboard, in any app, on Wayland or X11. Optionally polished by AI first.

# features

Everything you need to stop typing

Wisper does one job and stays out of your way. These are the controls you actually get.

  • The overlay that follows you

    A small pill floats at the bottom of whichever monitor your cursor is on. The voice-reactive waveform, a rotating radiant beam, and one button to cancel.

    No speech detected. Try speaking closer to the mic.

  • Hear it work

    Subtle sounds mark recording start and text ready. Master toggle plus per-event controls. Silence it whenever you want.

  • Push-to-talk

    Hold to record, release to transcribe. Prefer toggle? One tap. Any key or key combo works.

    F9

  • Nothing leaves

    Transcription runs on your machine. Cloud is strictly opt-in.

    0 bytes sent

  • Pick your microphone

    Choose a specific input device or stay on system default. Virtual sinks are filtered out. Test the level before you rely on it.

  • AI cleanup, your voice

    An optional pass reformats and polishes the transcript. It never answers for you. Six writing styles: Auto, Clean-up, Email, Developer, Messaging, Formal, plus custom instructions.

  • Your words, your way

    Custom vocabulary turns shortcuts into proper terms. Say "gpt", get "GPT". Scan past dictations to find new words worth saving.

  • Look back

    Searchable history: replay the recording, re-transcribe, edit, copy. Set retention limits. See typing time saved.

  • Local or cloud

    Stay fully offline, or plug in a cloud speech key: OpenAI, Groq, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Switch any time.

  • Out of the way

    Lives in the system tray. Close the window and it keeps running, ready for the next hotkey.

  • Updates itself

    Checks GitHub releases and installs new versions in-app, with signed auto-updates. No hunting for downloads.

  • Starts with you

    Launch at login, start hidden in the tray. First run walks you through the model download and push-to-talk vs toggle.

# setup

First-run setup

Three steps and you're dictating.

1 Pick an engine

On first launch, open Engine. Download a local ONNX model: Parakeet for breadth, Moonshine for speed (56 MB), or IndicConformer for Indian languages, or skip it and connect a cloud provider like OpenAI or Groq. Nothing transcribes until one is active.

Engine tab · local or cloud

2 Paste backend

Wisper inserts text by simulating a paste at your cursor. On Wayland, install ydotool for prompt-free pasting:

sudo systemctl enable --now ydotoold
sudo usermod -aG input $USER

wtype also works on compositors that support the Wayland virtual-keyboard protocol. enigo is built in but prompts for the RemoteDesktop portal once on Wayland.

Wayland · ydotool recommended

3 Pick your mic

In General settings, choose a specific input device or leave it on system default. Test the level before you rely on it.

General tab · input device

# faq

Questions people ask

The honest answers, including the Linux-specific rough edges.

Is my audio sent to the cloud?

No, not by default. Transcription runs on your machine with local ONNX models. Cloud providers are optional and only used if you add a key in Engine or Process settings.

Which Linux desktops are supported?

Any Linux with X11 or Wayland. Wisper uses a global hotkey that works the same in both. For pasting without a permission prompt on Wayland, install ydotool.

Why does it ask for "remote desktop" permission?

That prompt comes from the built-in paste tool (enigo) on Wayland, which routes input through the XDG Desktop Portal. Installing ydotool or wtype avoids it, because they inject input through dedicated channels below the compositor.

Do I need to download a model?

Only if you want to stay fully local. On first launch, open the Engine tab and pick a local ONNX model: Moonshine (56 MB) for speed, Parakeet for breadth, or IndicConformer for Indian languages. Prefer not to? Connect a cloud provider like OpenAI or Groq instead. Wisper can't transcribe until one engine is active, and you can cancel a download mid-way.

What is the cloud option?

In the Engine tab you can switch from the local model to a cloud speech provider (OpenAI, Groq, or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint) using a key you supply. The audio then leaves your machine to that provider. Everything else, including the AI cleanup step, works the same.

What does the AI cleanup actually change?

It re-formats and polishes the transcript through the chat model you configure: OpenAI, Anthropic, Groq, OpenRouter, Ollama, OpenCode Go, or your own endpoint. It rewrites only what you said, never answers for you. Profiles cover auto, cleanup, email, developer, messaging, and formal writing.

Is Wisper free and open source?

Yes. The code is MIT licensed and the repository is public on GitHub. There is no account, no telemetry, and no paid tier.
