# how it works

From voice to text in one breath

A short pipeline with no round trip to a server, the same on Linux, Windows, and macOS. You speak, a local model listens, your words land.

  1. Speak

    hotkey · push-to-talk / toggle

    Hold the global hotkey (Super on Linux, Win on Windows, Cmd on macOS) for push-to-talk, or tap it to toggle. Your mic records locally, and the overlay pill shows a live waveform plus the icon of the app you started in.

  2. Transcribe

    onnx · on-device · stays loaded

    Local ONNX models: Whisper, Parakeet, IndicConformer, Moonshine, SenseVoice, or Qwen3, with auto-detect or your own language priority. They turn speech into text on your machine, silence is trimmed, and models stay loaded so your next dictation starts instantly. Cloud keys (OpenAI, Groq, Sarvam) work too, strictly opt-in.

  3. Insert

    paste-back · origin app

    The text is pasted back into the app where you pressed the hotkey: ydotool, wtype, or the built-in tool on Wayland, direct typing on X11, Ctrl+V on Windows, Cmd+V on macOS (one Accessibility grant). Optionally polished by AI first.

# features

Everything you need to stop typing

Wisper does one job and stays out of your way. These are the controls you actually get.

  • presence

    The overlay that follows you

    A small pill floats on whichever monitor your cursor is on, pinned to the top or bottom, on Linux, Windows, and macOS alike. It shows the app you started dictating in, a voice-reactive waveform, a rotating radiant beam, and one button to cancel.

    No speech detected. Try speaking closer to the mic.

  • feedback

    Hear it work

    Subtle sounds mark recording start and text ready. Master toggle plus per-event controls. Silence it whenever you want.

  • control

    Push-to-talk

    Hold to record, release to transcribe. Prefer toggle? One tap. Any key or key combo works, and the Super key reads Win on Windows and Cmd on macOS.

    F9

  • privacy

    Nothing leaves

    Transcription runs on your machine. Cloud is strictly opt-in, and keys are saved on this device only.

    0 bytes sent

  • refine

    AI cleanup, your voice

    An optional pass reformats and polishes the transcript. It never answers for you. Six writing styles: Auto, Clean-up, Email, Developer, Messaging, Formal, plus custom instructions. Runs on OpenAI, Anthropic, Groq, OpenRouter, Ollama, OpenCode Go, or your own endpoint.

  • vocabulary

    Your words, your way

    Custom vocabulary turns shortcuts into proper terms. Say "gpt", get "GPT". 4 dictionary profiles (developer, email, messaging, general) with import/export, URL sync, and per-profile toggle. Scan past dictations to find new words worth saving, or let it auto-scan.

  • input

    Pick your microphone

    Choose a specific input device or stay on system default. Virtual sinks are filtered out, and you test the level before you rely on it. Windows and macOS ask for microphone permission on first run. Reduce background noise with a strength slider, and trim silence before transcription.

  • memory

    Look back

    Searchable history: replay the recording, re-transcribe, edit, copy. Collapsible Today shows today dictations/words/time saved. Set retention limits and choose whether recordings are kept.

  • engines

    Local or cloud

    Stay fully offline, or plug in a cloud speech key: OpenAI, Groq, Sarvam, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Add several keys per provider and Wisper rotates past rate limits automatically. Switch any time.

  • paste-back

    Back where you started

    Wisper remembers the window where you pressed the hotkey and pastes there, even if you click elsewhere mid-sentence. The pill shows that app's icon. Native on Windows and macOS; on GNOME Wayland a tiny helper extension does it (bundled, no sudo).

  • languages

    Your language order

    Pick transcription languages yourself or leave auto-detect on. Enable several and drag to set priority, so a bilingual mix picks the right one first.

  • tray

    Out of the way

    Lives in the system tray on all three OSes. Copy last transcription without opening the window. Close the window and it keeps running. Ctrl+W hides it, Ctrl+Q quits (Cmd+W / Cmd+Q on macOS).

  • fresh

    Updates itself

    Checks GitHub releases and installs in-app (About → Check for updates, also sidebar banner), with signature-verified updates. deb, rpm, AppImage, Windows installer, and macOS DMG all update from the same stable channel.

  • launch

    Starts with you

    Launch at login, start hidden in the tray. First run walks you through the model download and push-to-talk vs toggle, on every OS.

# setup

First-run setup

Three steps and you're dictating, on Linux, Windows, or macOS.

1 Pick an engine

On first launch, open Engine. Download a local ONNX model: Whisper (100 languages), Parakeet (25), IndicConformer (22 Indian languages), Moonshine (56 MB, fastest), SenseVoice (Chinese, Japanese, Korean), or Qwen3 (30 languages). Or skip it and connect a cloud provider: OpenAI, Groq, Sarvam (Transcribe, Translate, Verbatim, Transliterate, Code-mix), or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint. Same choices on all three OSes. Nothing transcribes until one is active.

Engine tab · local or cloud · Linux, Windows, macOS

2 Paste backend

Wisper pastes text back into the app where you started dictating. What you need depends on your OS:

  • Linux, Wayland: install ydotool for prompt-free pasting:
sudo systemctl enable --now ydotoold
sudo usermod -aG input $USER
  • Linux, X11: nothing to install, direct typing works out of the box.
  • Windows: nothing to install, built-in paste with Ctrl+V.
  • macOS: nothing to install, built-in paste with Cmd+V. Grant Accessibility once: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility → enable Wisper. After each update, remove Wisper with minus and re-add it with plus, or the hotkey stops firing.

wtype also works on compositors that support the Wayland virtual-keyboard protocol. The built-in tool may ask for the RemoteDesktop portal once on Wayland; ydotool avoids prompts entirely.

ydotool 1.0.4 or newer is what Wisper expects: older builds ignore the fast typing flags and type about 40x slower. The full ydotool setup guide covers 1.0.4+ on Arch, Fedora, and Debian/Ubuntu.

Wayland: ydotool or wtype · X11: built-in · Windows: built-in · macOS: Accessibility grant

3 Pick your mic

In General settings, choose a specific input device or leave it on system default. Test the level before you rely on it. Permission differs per OS: macOS asks for Microphone access on first run, Windows needs Settings → Privacy → Microphone access allowed (when blocked it feeds silence and the meter stays flat), Linux uses PipeWire/PulseAudio with no extra grant.

General tab · input device · all three OSes

# faq

Questions people ask

The honest answers, including the platform-specific rough edges.

How do I install on Linux?

Pick your package from the download section above: deb for Debian/Ubuntu, rpm for Fedora, or the AppImage for everything else (run chmod +x Wisper-*.AppImage first). On Wayland, install ydotool (1.0.4 or newer) afterwards for prompt-free pasting. The ydotool setup guide covers 1.0.4+ on Arch, Fedora, and Debian/Ubuntu.

How do I install on Windows?

Grab the NSIS installer from the download section above and run it. Stable releases update in-app automatically.

Why is my mic silent on Windows?

Almost always the privacy switch: open Settings → Privacy → Microphone access and allow apps to use the microphone. When blocked, Windows feeds silence instead of an error, so the mic meter in General stays flat. The built-in Test paste button helps confirm everything past the mic is working.

How do I install on macOS?

Grab the DMG for your chip (Apple Silicon or Intel) from the download section above, drag Wisper to Applications, then right-click + Open on first launch: the build is unsigned, so macOS needs that explicit open once. Then grant Microphone and Accessibility access when asked. If the hotkey ever stops working after an update, remove Wisper with the minus button in Accessibility and re-add it with plus. macOS 13 or newer, Apple Silicon and Intel.

Is my audio sent to the cloud?

No, not by default. Transcription runs on your machine with local ONNX models. Cloud providers are optional and only used if you add a key in Engine or Process settings.

Does Wisper work on Windows?

Yes. Windows ships as an NSIS installer with the same local transcription, overlay, and paste flow as Linux. Audio uses WASAPI and the overlay sits above the taskbar. If the mic meter stays flat, check Settings → Privacy → Microphone access: Windows delivers silence when access is blocked.

Which Linux desktops are supported?

Any Linux with X11 or Wayland. Wisper uses a global hotkey that works the same in both. For pasting without a permission prompt on Wayland, install ydotool. On GNOME Wayland, paste-back-to-origin installs a tiny bundled helper extension automatically (window list and jump back only, no sudo); other desktops (KDE, Hyprland, Sway, X11) work with no helper.

Why does it ask for "remote desktop" permission?

That prompt comes from the built-in paste tool (enigo) on Wayland, which routes input through the XDG Desktop Portal. Installing ydotool or wtype avoids it, because they inject input through dedicated channels below the compositor.

Do I need to download a model?

Only if you want to stay fully local. On first launch, open the Engine tab and pick a local ONNX model: Moonshine (56 MB) for speed, Whisper for 100 languages, Parakeet for 25, or IndicConformer for Indian languages, plus SenseVoice and Qwen3. Prefer not to? Connect a cloud provider (OpenAI, Groq, Sarvam, or custom) instead. Wisper can't transcribe until one engine is active, and you can cancel a download mid-way.

What is the cloud option?

In the Engine tab you can switch from the local model to a cloud speech provider (OpenAI, Groq, Sarvam, or a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint) using keys you supply. Sarvam adds five output modes: Transcribe, Translate to English, Verbatim, Transliterate, and Code-mix. You can store several keys per provider; Wisper rotates past rate limits and server errors automatically, and skips a key that comes back unauthorized. The audio then leaves your machine to that provider. Everything else, including the AI cleanup step, works the same, and the Process tab has a cost estimator if you want to see monthly spend before you commit.

Which languages can I dictate in?

Whatever your model covers: Whisper handles 100 languages, Qwen3 30, Parakeet 25, SenseVoice 5, and IndicConformer covers up to 22 Indian languages. Cloud providers follow their own support. In General settings you can leave language detection on auto, or enable several languages and drag them into priority order.

How do overlays, tray, and updates differ per OS?

They don't, much. The overlay pill, system tray, launch-at-login, history, and in-app updates work the same on Linux, Windows, and macOS. Windows pastes with Ctrl+V and macOS with Cmd+V; the hotkey keycap reads Super on Linux, Win on Windows, Cmd on macOS.

What does the AI cleanup actually change?

It re-formats and polishes the transcript through the chat model you configure: OpenAI, Anthropic, Groq, OpenRouter, Ollama, OpenCode Go, or your own endpoint. It rewrites only what you said, never answers for you. Profiles cover auto, cleanup, email, developer, messaging, and formal writing.

Is Wisper free and open source?

Yes. The code is MIT licensed and the repository is public on GitHub. There is no account, no telemetry, and no paid tier.
